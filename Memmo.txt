Design Memo:

Since a tree structure clearly shows an order, it seems sense to use it for the doctor reporting system.  Every doctor may have workers, and those employees may also have their own reports.  Since each doctor (node) can have two reports (left and right), a binary tree is a good fit for this concept.  Using a tree to navigate, edit, and visualize connections is also simple.  For example, you may simply follow that branch down the tree if you need to see every patient who sees a particular doctor.
There are many reasons why the preorder, inorder, and postorder traversal techniques are beneficial.  When you want to see the root doctor first, preorder works well because it prints the structure from the top down.  When you need data in a particular order, such as sorting or displaying in a binary search tree from smallest to largest, inorder is frequently utilized.  Postorder, which is essentially working from the bottom up, is useful when you need to process or remove child nodes before their parents.
On the other hand, piles are ideal for a situation similar to an emergency line.  Without having to filter the complete list, the system can always access the patient with the most urgent need first thanks to a min-heap feature.  Because of this, it is quick and effective, which is crucial for systems in real time like hospitals.  As more patients are added, the heap automatically rearranges itself to replicate how real-world triage systems operate, always ensuring that the most urgent cases are taken care of first.
